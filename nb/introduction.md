# Introduction
Electrophysiology has the problem of the Beginners Curse. There is a pretty steep learning curve for both being able to do experiments and analyze experiments. The steeper the learning curve the worse your outcomes at the beginning (thus the curse). To help people get over that hill faster I thought I would write a modern electrophysiology handbook. I spent a lot of time finding resources and wasted a lot of time on bad resources or pulling snippets from different sources. While there are many books on electrophysiology none of them cover electrophysiology in a way that I felt really pushed for a solid foundation of experimental design and data analysis. Lastly, to push the envelope of your experimental design I believe that you must embrace some custom analysis. Your experiments are only as good as your analysis. With the NIH looking to push computational modeling it is even more important to really understand the data you collect.

This book covers design of experiments, biology behind why we do certain experiments, analyzing the data you will get from experiments and the interpretation of data.

Throughout the book you may notice that some plots are interactive and others are not. Many of the non-interactive plots are there to reduce the size of the HTML pages which unfortunately leads to inconsistent formating. Interactive plots contain data that you should interact with.

There are a lot of opinions in electrophysiology and lots of protocols where people do not understand why they are doing what they do. For example I had a manuscript under review where the reviewer stated that you must use series resistance compensation for current recordings (not true) and another stated the mEPSCs essentially mean nothing (also not true). There are actually two acceptable ways to control for series resistance. There are caveats to interpreting mEPSCs but, mEPSCs are still very useful for examining the amount of exicitory synaptic input onto a cell even with these caveats. With this book I tried to put forth accepted ways to run, analyze and interpret data. Some of this book is intended to provide you with the analytic skills to make decisions yourself because at the end of the day you can interpret your data however you want, it is just a matter of convincing reviewers that your interpretation is correct.
