# Ligand gated channels
Ligand gated channels are the primary way neurons signal to each other. The most common neurotransmitters are GABA and glutamate. The are some other neurotransmitters that have ion channels such as acetylcholinea and glycine.

## AMPA receptors
AMPA receptors are the primary excitatory glutamatergic receptor in the CNS. AMPA receptors are heterotrimeric that can be composed of four different subunits; Gria1, Gria2, Gria3 and Gria4. You may also see AMPA subunits refered to as GluA or GluR. AMPARs are primarily permeable to Na+. AMPARs lacking Gria2 are permeable to Ca++ and K+. Subunits composition can change over development. Additionally, early in development many postsynaptic sites lack AMPARs. These are called silent synapses. AMPAR subunit composition differs across celltypes with PV interneurons having a particularly high number of receptors lacking the Gria2 (GluR2 or GluA2)

## NMDA receptors
NMDA receptors are an excitatory glutamatergic and glycinergic receptor. NMDARs cannot open unless Mg++ is removed from the pore which typically occurs at high voltages (10+ mV). NMDARs are primarily permeable to Ca++.

## Nicotinic acetylcholine receptors
Nicotinic acetycholine receptors (nAChRs) are pentameric proteins composed of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ subunits. nAChRs can be activated by acetylcholine and nicotine hence their name.
